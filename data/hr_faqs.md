# Temple University HR Frequently Asked Questions

## Q1: How long do I have to wait before I can use tuition remission?

Tuition remission benefits begin the first day of the semester after your hire date or the date you became a full-time employee. For example, if you are hired in October, you would be eligible starting the following spring semester.

## Q2: How many credits can I take per semester with tuition remission?

Full-time faculty and staff can take up to eight (8) credits per semester for undergraduate or graduate courses at 100 percent tuition remission.

## Q3: Is my spouse eligible for tuition remission?

Spouses of tenured, tenure-track faculty, faculty "grandfathered" as Special Appointment Faculty, and spouses of regular full-time administrative staff at grade T-26 and above are eligible for 50 percent tuition remission for eligible undergraduate and graduate courses, beginning the first semester after the employee's hire date.

## Q4: Are graduate tuition benefits taxable?

Yes. Under current tax law, the value of employee graduate tuition benefits in excess of $5,250 will be included in the employee's taxable wages, and all applicable taxes will be withheld during the year the tuition benefits are received. Spousal and domestic partner tuition benefits, both undergraduate and graduate, are also considered taxable.

## Q5: What happens if I miss my tuition payment deadline or have a past-due balance?

You may not use tuition remission benefits unless your account is current or you have made arrangements to pay any past due balance in full by the end of the current semester. Forms authorizing Temple University to advance the outstanding balance and deduct the amount in equal payroll installments are available in the Bursar's Office.

## Q6: Can my child use tuition remission? How many semesters are covered?

Legally dependent children of eligible employees who are under 24 years of age at initial enrollment are eligible for tuition remission for up to 10 semesters to complete a degree in a Temple University undergraduate program. If hired before July 1, 2014, the benefit is 100% from day one. If hired on or after July 1, 2014, the benefit is progressive: 50% in year one, 75% in year two, and 100% after three years of service.

## Q7: Can I use tuition remission for Law School, Medical School, or Dental School?

No. No tuition remission is given for courses taken in Temple University's schools of Law, Medicine, Dentistry, or Podiatric Medicine, with the exception of specially designated graduate courses as determined by the Dean's Offices of the specific professional schools. In the School of Pharmacy, tuition remission is limited to undergraduate programs.

## Q8: How many vacation days do I get per year?

For non-bargaining employees at salary grade levels T21-T25: 15 days per year after completing one year of service, increasing to 20 days after 12 years. For T26 and above: 20 days per year. Effective July 1, 2023, employees with 15 or more years of service receive an additional week (up to 25 days). Non-bargaining employees may roll over up to half of their annual vacation entitlement each fiscal year.

## Q9: How many sick days do I receive?

Each non-bargaining, full-time employee receives 10 sick days per year. During the introductory/probationary period, sick days accrue at the rate of one day per month. Unused sick days are contributed to a paid sick leave bank at the end of the year, which can be used in subsequent years for your own illness. There is no cap on the total number of sick days you may bank. Employees who retire on or after July 1, 2023, with at least 100 banked days will be paid out for 30 of those days.

## Q10: How much bereavement leave am I entitled to?

Paid bereavement leave for non-bargaining employees is as follows: 4 days for spouse, parent, child, dependent stepchild, sister, or brother; 3 days for grandparent, grandchild, father-in-law, or mother-in-law; 1 day for grandparent-in-law, sister-in-law, brother-in-law, son-in-law, or daughter-in-law. Bereavement leave may be taken between the date of passing and the day after the ceremony/service/burial.

## Q11: Am I eligible for Family and Medical Leave (FMLA)?

To be eligible for FMLA, you must have worked 1,250 hours during the past year and have 12 months of service over the past seven years. Qualified employees may take up to 12 workweeks of leave to care for a newborn or newly adopted child, to recuperate from a serious health condition, to care for a seriously ill family member, or for a qualifying military exigency. FMLA leave may be taken on a continuous, intermittent, or reduced-hour basis. Contact the Benefits Office at 215-926-2282 for more information.

## Q12: What is the university's policy on weapons on campus?

Temple University prohibits all persons (except authorized police officers and law enforcement personnel) from possessing, carrying, storing, or using weapons on university property. This includes firearms, knives, fireworks, ammunition, explosives, and replicas. Violations may result in removal from university property, discharge, arrest, and/or criminal prosecution. This applies to all work locations including offices, work sites, vehicles, and field locations.

## Q13: Can I work a second job outside of Temple?

Outside employment is not prohibited, but hours worked in outside employment must not coincide with your hours of work for the university. Outside employment must not detract from your work responsibilities or create a conflict of interest. You may not receive income from outside entities for materials produced or services rendered while performing your university job. Administrators must use personal or vacation days for consulting. Faculty may devote an average of one day per calendar week toward consulting with prior Dean approval.

## Q14: What is the drug and alcohol testing policy?

Temple requires pre-employment drug screening for all new hires; offers are contingent on being drug-free. The university may also require reasonable-suspicion testing when there is evidence of drug or alcohol influence at work, including erratic conduct, on-the-job accidents suggesting impairment, or excessive unexplained absenteeism. Employees who test positive are subject to disciplinary action. Employees must report any criminal drug conviction to Human Resources within five days.

## Q15: How do I report an ethics violation or workplace misconduct?

You have several options: (1) report to your immediate supervisor or higher-level management, (2) contact the Ethics and Compliance Helpline at 844-755-3394 or online at https://secure.ethicspoint.com/domain/media/en/gui/61440/index.html (available 24/7, reports may be anonymous), or (3) contact the Human Resources Department at 215-926-2298. Temple University prohibits retaliation against anyone who reports suspected violations in good faith.
